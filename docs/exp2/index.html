How to read the picture

  • Each word is a shape. Blue circles are the first word of a pair; orange squares are the second word. (You can rename these groups under “Display options”.)
  • At first you see all words. Words that have similar partners sit near each other.
  • Click a word and it moves to the middle, with its partners around it. The closer a partner, the stronger the link. Faint grey words on the outside are other words of the same group that share some of those partners.
  • Click a line to read example sentences for that pair (if the data has any).
  • To go back, press “Back to all words” or click an empty spot in the picture.

The two kinds of strength

  • Mutual strength (technically “symmetric association”): how tightly the two words are tied together, whichever way round you read the pair. Higher means tighter.
  • One-way pull (technically “asymmetric association”): a direction-sensitive score for how strongly one word of the pair points to the other. If the data has a score for each direction, the picture uses the one for the word you are looking at (click a word of the first group and it is the pull of that word on its partners; click a word of the second group and it is the pull the other way). Exactly which measure was used depends on whoever prepared the data.

Use the “Distance shows” buttons above the picture to choose which of the two decides how far each partner sits from the middle.
